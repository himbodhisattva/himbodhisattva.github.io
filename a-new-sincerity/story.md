# A New Sincerity story structure

## Opening

*Somehow Both a Victory Lap and a Cry for Help* supplies the title and the
organizing phrase. The opening post explains the premise: the author invented a
counterfactual version of himself who kept writing emo and post-hardcore songs,
then asked that version to remake old recordings.

## The first record

*(Unfortunately) I Know Exactly What I'm Doing* comes first. It tests what the
counterfactual musician would write now: pop-punk pushed toward hyperpop, with
sincerity and self-absorption turned up.

The album appears with its complete track list and a collage of simulated
reviews. The collage identifies the lines as emulations and states that the
named publications did not publish them. Its Stereogum line, “somehow both a cry
for help and a victory lap,” becomes the title of the later record.

## The older songs

*Revisionist History* follows. A New Sincerity remakes songs originally written
for Lock Haven and The System of the World. Its complete track list appears in
the essay.

“Like They Do In The Movies” receives a direct comparison. The version labeled
as the reconstruction is already an AI recovery from a recording where the
original performance is barely audible. The later remake emphasizes the high,
harmonized guitar arpeggios and gives the chorus more room.

## Friday Girls

The next embedded post supplies the cause: the author's daughters said that his
sad love songs were not real love songs. Friday Girls became a way to write
uncomplicated ones. A New Sincerity then remixed “Since When?!”

The section shows the Friday Girls cover and complete track list. “wait, you
like me too?!” appears only as a small aside.

## The feedback loop

The final essay section combines the feedback-loop argument in one passage. It
explains that introspection changes its subject. AI shortens the path from
interpretation to song, from song to lived experience, and from experience to
another version of the author. Knowing that the process is constructed does not
cancel the attachment or restore a neutral viewpoint.

The section distinguishes two related moves. *(Unfortunately)* uses recursive
self-awareness. Friday Girls uses recursive self-creation. An older lyric from
“The Top of the Mountain” shows that the limits of explanation were already
present in the earlier writing. The section closes by requiring the next song
to reveal something the author did not know before writing it.

## Public context

Four tweet groups follow the essay. They preserve the original post and reply
relationships for the hyperreality comment, the self-deception joke, the
Debord/Wallace/Fisher premise and “their mistake” reply, and the “thinking is
dangerous” exchange. The reply about inventing people must never appear without
the post it answers.

## Catalog link

One link closes the page. It combines “the records.” with the smaller line “get
in loser, we're subverting postmodernism from the inside.”

On `/records/`, the album shelf stays visible above the selected album. Selecting
an album scrolls to its full detail and track list; direct album links do the
same. Album playback continues through the record, and the compact lyric view
tracks the current line.
