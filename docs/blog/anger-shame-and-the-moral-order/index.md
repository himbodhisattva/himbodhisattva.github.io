# what anger and shame are for

I saw a screenshot of a neighbor objecting to a seven-year-old drawing with chalk on the public sidewalk. the first message was a precious request that she draw somewhere else; the second cited municipal ordinances and threatened to report her. [I said I was enraged by the first message](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2079007467447652808).

someone asked a good question: why repost things that enrage you?

I've gone through a few cycles of thinking, ah, now I'm a stable, emotionally mature person, only to discover a big "some situations excluded" caveat. parenthood is very good at finding those situations. what looked like emotional transcendence was sometimes just having nobody vulnerable whom I was obligated to defend. anger on my own behalf can often be relinquished. protective anger cannot always be dissolved without betraying what has been entrusted to me.

the first message revealed a moral world I hate: one in which the ordinary presence of children in shared space is treated as an intrusion. the second revealed the enforcement mechanism. the neighbor wanted the benefits of a family neighborhood while demanding that actual children remain visually and acoustically absent.

[anger is a detector and an enforcer](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2079222933692830070). it detects rejection and trespass, especially boundary violations. it assigns responsibility and mobilizes resistance. sometimes anger is how a moral perception arrives. refusing to engage with it is not necessarily transcendence; often it is refusing to see what you have seen.

detectors have false positives. enforcers can be disproportionate. social media can turn enforcement into theater, or make violations gratifying to seek out. the work is to let anger complete its movement into a clear judgment and a proportionate boundary instead of turning it into either shame or entertainment. reposting is healthy when it clarifies and ratifies a norm. marinating in rage is not. [that was the useful answer buried in my ramble](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2079218762549727727).

therapy culture likes to say that all emotions are valid, but edits out the ones whose information challenges its moral assumptions. [it rejects much more than shame](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2078346409913577760). it combines real truths about trauma with feminine moral norms, then presents the package as psychologically neutral.

imagine a therapist telling a bullied boy to remember his training and choke the bully out the next time he is touched. restoring agency and status, including publicly demonstrating that you are not a target, are important parts of healing.

this is deliberately shocking. violence is not always wise or justified, and a therapist should not casually prescribe it. but the hypothetical reveals the boundary of what therapy culture can perceive. humiliation is not always an internal self-esteem problem. it can be a fact in the world. someone dominated you, other people saw it, and your place in a social order changed. healing may require something to be righted there. redescribing it inside your head may not be enough.

shame is where this becomes clearest. [there are at least three different experiences hiding under the word](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2078352918567440529):

1. deficiency shame — a child is made to feel defective for having needs or traits, or through abuse and neglect. therapy culture understands this one well.
2. weakness shame — someone can dominate or humiliate you because you lacked strength, courage, or competence. the victimhood may be unearned while the lack is real.
3. character shame — your behavior fell below a standard you rightly hold. the judgment is accurate.

treating all three as the first type makes us unable to respond to the other two. weakness becomes a self-esteem problem instead of a demand to become harder to dominate. character failure becomes "internalized shame" instead of a demand to repair what you did. a moral system that cannot distinguish abusive shame from accurate shame eventually loses the ability to judge anything.

I've been using the phrase "Kegan stage 5 honor culture" for the alternative. Kegan's stages are useful shorthand here. at stage 3, the honor code is subject: it is the unquestioned social world you inhabit. these cultures can be brutal. at stage 4, you can take the code as object and author your own values. honor and shame now look like primitive, violent, patriarchal methods of social control.

but self-authored morality can be reward-hacked. every standard that finds me lacking can be dismissed as coercion; every painful judgment can be redescribed as internalized shame. at stage 5, autonomous morality itself becomes object. you can knowingly yoke yourself to an honor code and let a community or tradition find you lacking. not because you never questioned the code, but because you did, and chose answerability over the nihilism of permanent self-exemption. [this is why stage 3 and stage 5 can look identical from stage 4](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2078379207273619647).

Judaism is a mature example. when we say *avinu malkeinu* on Yom Kippur, we stand publicly before the highest possible audience, and with our community, and say: we have failed. the standard remains valid. judge us, but as our Father, help us return.

that is honor-shame morality in an extraordinarily developed form: confession without permanent disgrace, judgment tempered by mercy, obligations of repair, and restoration through *teshuvah*. [we do not escape shame by declaring the standard oppressive; we answer it by becoming worthy again](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2078480388671049889).

the system is matryoshka-shaped. later stages contain the earlier ones rather than deleting them. adults may be capable of choosing their commitments reflectively, but [it is neglectful to expect children, including teenagers, to live in a stage 4 society](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2078567575643205889). they deserve stage 3 systems that provide meaning in a more fundamental and unavoidable way. in the loose developmental sense, [ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny](https://x.com/himbodhisattva/status/2078678337128685945). maturation runs through forms that cultural development also ran through.

this brings us back to children drawing on the sidewalk. a living moral order is reproduced by families, and families produce beings who are noisy, dependent, unreasonable, and not yet self-authoring. a society that makes no room for their chalk is not more mature. it is living off inherited social capital while refusing the process that creates more of it.

the same evasion appears in our relationship to violence. accumulated wealth and social stability rest, at the final layer, on the state's capacity for coercion and its implicit threat of violence. that is not the whole causal explanation of order. most order is maintained proximally by legitimacy, habit, reciprocity, affection, shame, and voluntary compliance. violence is the final guarantor.

the default western outlook, which I think of as atheistic Protestantism, imagines itself universalist and pacifist. but westerners plainly authorize enormous state violence. what they reject is personal coercion close enough to feel morally responsible for. they sanctify distant, procedural coercion: the ordinance, the report, the official who eventually arrives. violence is acceptable once it has been bureaucratically laundered and made emotionally deniable.

this is the pattern connecting anger, shame, honor, and violence. we want the goods produced by boundaries while treating anger as dysregulation. we want standards while treating accurate shame as abuse. we want social order while pretending it has no final enforcer. we want living neighborhoods while treating children as a nuisance.

the mature move is not to maximize any of these things. anger can be mistaken. shame can be sadistic. honor cultures can be cruel. violence can destroy the order it claims to protect. each has a proper place. anger should become judgment and proportionate action. shame should distinguish what needs repair and allow standing to be restored. honor should be chosen answerability. coercion should be bounded by legitimacy and used as a last resort.

emotional maturity does not mean becoming the kind of person who never needs anger. moral maturity does not mean building a world that never judges. both mean learning what these faculties are for.
